# Router: partial re-route from an earlier route (`--reuse`, 2026-11-01)

Goal (user-approved 2026-11-01): the router starts from an earlier routed result and routes again only what the part
moves affect, the way a person fixes a board after a part move. Design: DESIGN.md section 5c. Branch
`claude/router-reuse` (on main 2d783a67). Scratch evidence (not tracked):
`/private/tmp/claude-501/-Users-pacosw-code-iot-lab/f7557682-4961-4da4-8f78-b3604f97dd7d/scratchpad/reuse/`
(`c1`..`c4/` cases: `place/` moved board, `reuse/` and `full/` run.py results, `*.log`; `gates/<case>-<mode>.txt` and
evidence folders; `det1`, `det2` determinism runs; `noreg-*` no-regression runs; `comp/s1` compaction step;
`prun1/` + `prun1.raw` placer run; `tools/` scripts: `mkcase.py`, `case.sh`, `gate.sh`, `noreg.sh`, `movedfrac.py`,
`placer-run.sh`).

Verdict source: KiCad DRC with `--schematic-parity` on the written board (run.py) and `node tools/gate.mjs NET-CORE
--fresh` in detached origin/main worktrees (`reuse/g1`, `reuse/g2`), the routed board copied into the project path. The
router's own claim is not used.

## Repair-like cases (NET-CORE)

Base: the passing step 0 board of the PR #79 run (`pfix/run1/step-0/route-0`, s 0.75, 1485.6 mm, 286 vias, DRC 0 / 0 / 0,
gate PASS). Each case moves 1-2 units (or one block) of its placement board (`step-0/b`) to the nearest legal pose
(`Evaluator.legal_penalty_at` = 0 on the 0.25 mm legalizer grid, `mkcase.py`, the PR #79 read arguments). Reuse =
`run.py --pcb MOVED --reuse step-0/route-0/NET-CORE.kicad_pcb --reuse-fallback none`; full = `run.py --pcb MOVED`.
Both modes of all cases ran at the same time (9 run.py processes and 2 gates on the Mac): the times are under load,
the ratio is the comparison.

Speed: core 3.7-11.8 x, run.py 3.0-5.2 x. The run.py rest (model.py 3 s, seed 1 s, write.py with zone refill 8 s,
DRC 4 s) is about 16 s per route and does not change with reuse. With 4 processes and no other load the reuse core
took 9 / 22 / 27 / 28 s (`reuse/q/*.n.log`, same seeds); the length recovery is 5-21 s of it (12-26 nets in scope).
Not seconds for every case: a case where the ripped nets conflict with kept nets negotiates for some iterations (c2: 7).

Length (honest): every reuse result is longer than the full route of the same moved board, by 2.4-15.7 mm (0.2-1.1 %),
with 3-9 fewer vias. Cause: 46-55 of 58 routed nets keep their step 0 copper, so the reuse result stays at the quality
of the step 0 route (1485.6 mm): c1 +3.1, c2 -9.2, c3 +3.4, c4 +4.9 mm against it. The full route negotiates all nets
again from a new start and lands on another end state: here -12.6, -16.1, +1.0, -4.8 mm against step 0. The difference
is the spread of the negotiation, not a defect of the kept copper; per net, the largest reuse excess is on kept or
neighbour nets that the full route moved (c1: VDD_CORE +8.2, /ETH_RXD0 +5.3; c4: the NAND nets of the moved block
+1.9 to +6.0, routed against kept copper). The repair compares keys with the length term, so a longer reuse result
can reject a proposal that a full route would accept on length; it cannot accept an illegal one.

Selection checks seen in the logs: c3 rips /NAND_HOLD and /NAND_MISO because their kept tracks now break the
clearance to the moved C24 pads (fixed-rule conflict), c4 rips /LED_STATUS for the same reason; GND (and VDD_3V3 in
c4) re-fan only the dropped clusters (1-9). Zero-move check: the step 0 route as seed with no moved part keeps all
56 nets, converges in iteration 1, and writes the same 970 tracks / 270 vias (core 2 s).

## Compaction steps: not used

Parts that move between consecutive compaction steps (`movedfrac.py`): PR #79 run 50-52 % of all 129 parts (65-67:
every movable unit), rank-by-length run 51-63 %; nets with a pad on a moved part: 22-30 % of all 165 nets, which is
most of the 77 routed nets. Measured: step 1 of the PR #79 run routed with reuse from step 0 (`comp/s1`): 65 parts
moved, core 131 s, 25 iterations, DRC 0 / 0 / 0, 1555.2 mm / 302 vias, against the full route of step 1 (core 119 s,
1 open /NAND_WP, 1465.6 mm / 297 vias). Not faster, and 90 mm longer: the compaction loop keeps the full route. (The
reuse result was legal where the full route left one open; one case, not a reason to change the rule.)

## Remaining gaps

1. Length recovery in scope costs 5-21 s of a 9-48 s reuse core: one strict A* per net and round plus a full pour
   check after each kept net (v9 gap 2). A per-layer pour check would make the reuse route closer to "seconds".
2. A plane net in conflict after iteration 1 fans out every pad again (full-route behaviour). Per-pad re-fan in every
   iteration would keep more GND copper; not needed by the cases so far.
3. The reuse length stays at the earlier route's quality; it does not search a new end state. For a final board, a full
   route (or a reuse route with a wider `--reuse-near`) is the comparison.
